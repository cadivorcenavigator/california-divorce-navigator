Things worth knowing

Practical starting points for understanding the process, communicating effectively, and staying organized. The things I wish someone had told me.

Understanding the process
👥

Who does what — and when

Attorneys, mediators, private judges, parenting coordinators, custody evaluators, therapists, and financial experts all play different roles. Using the wrong professional for the wrong problem is one of the most expensive mistakes people make.

🏛️

Court, mediation, or settlement — understanding your options

Not everything ends in trial. Many cases resolve through mediation, negotiated settlement, or court-mandated agreements. Understanding what each path costs — in time, money, and outcome — changes how you approach decisions.

📋

Legal custody, physical custody & parenting plans

Legal custody determines who makes decisions about a child's medical care, education, and upbringing. Physical custody determines where the child lives and the timeshare arrangement. A parenting plan spells out the details — schedules, exchanges, holidays, decision protocols. Understanding the difference, and what's actually negotiable, matters before you're sitting across a table from a mediator.

🔍

Custody evaluations and assessments

Full evaluations, brief focused assessments, and recommending mediators each serve different purposes at different stages. A full evaluation involves a court-appointed or privately retained evaluator conducting interviews, home visits, and collateral contacts. A brief focused assessment addresses a specific issue. A recommending mediator builds a custody agreement and parenting plan. Knowing which process applies to your situation — and how to prepare — matters.

Communication
💬

Writing messages that don't hurt you

In high-conflict situations every written communication is potential evidence. Tone, timing, and content matter. The goal is clear, documented, and neutral — not emotional or retaliatory.

📱

Co-parenting apps — OurFamilyWizard, TalkingParents, Coparently

What each does, when courts prefer them, and why a structured communication platform can reduce conflict and create a useful record.

👨‍👧

Talking with your kids

Age-appropriate honesty, keeping them out of the middle, how to discuss the other parent. When kids need more support than a parent can give, a therapist — not another adult confidant — is the right call.

Organization & documentation
📊

What to document and how

The incidents, communications, and patterns that matter. Contemporaneous records carry weight. Retroactive summaries usually don't.

📋

Before speaking with an attorney

What to gather, how to organize your timeline, and how to prepare focused questions. A prepared client is a more effective client — and spends less per billable hour.

Assets, support & housing
💰

Thinking about settlement vs. litigation

When it makes sense to settle, what mediation can actually achieve, and how to evaluate offers without the pressure of the moment clouding the long-term picture.

🏠

RSUs, assets & community property in California

California is a community property state. Tech compensation, business interests, real estate — understanding the basics of how assets are treated helps you ask the right questions of your attorney and financial advisor.

Co-parenting
↔️

Co-parenting vs. parallel parenting

When high conflict makes traditional co-parenting impossible, parallel parenting lowers contact and clarifies boundaries. Many families in contested cases need this framework and don't know it exists.

📅

Holiday and summer schedules that hold

Common structures, how to handle ambiguity, and what to put in writing before the conflict starts — not after.
