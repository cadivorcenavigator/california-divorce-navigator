# The Closed-Loop Co-Parenting Playbook

## A field guide for reading difficult messages, answering the real request, and preserving a clear record

This playbook is educational and is not legal advice. Your order and local law control. Ask qualified counsel about rights, deadlines, safety, emergencies, or possible violations.

## The goal

The goal is not to win a debate inside the messaging app. The goal is to leave a clean record showing:

- what the child-focused issue was;
- what information was available;
- what you agreed to, declined, needed, or completed;
- which rule controlled, if one was relevant; and
- that collateral pressure did not replace a decision.

## The ten-minute method

### 1. Regulate before you interpret

Unless there is a genuine safety emergency, do not draft from the notification preview. Read once, step away, and return with the order map available. A strong emotional reaction may be completely understandable and still be a poor drafting tool.

### 2. Identify the smallest concrete decision

Finish this sentence:

> The sender needs me to decide, provide, confirm, or do ________ by ________.

If you cannot fill the blanks, the request is not yet clear. Ask for the missing question, date, document, cost, provider recommendation, or proposed schedule.

### 3. Separate substance from procedure

Ask two questions:

1. What response, notice, or timing rule applies?
2. Who has authority to make the underlying decision?

A duty to respond is not necessarily a duty to agree. A label does not automatically create a payment obligation, schedule change, joint-decision outcome, or emergency.

### 4. Decompose the message

Sort each component:

| Bucket | What belongs here | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Answer | Actual child-focused requests | Give a disposition |
| Verify | Facts, documents, costs, deadlines, order text | Check or request support |
| Ignore | Motive claims, insults, broad history, moral judgments | Do not rebut unless materially consequential |
| Separate | New, unrelated decisions | Require a separate request |

### 5. Classify the communication style

- **Collaborative:** one issue, adequate facts, real choice, no threat.
- **Directive:** asserted obligation, accusation, command, or consequence.
- **Conflated:** several decisions plus history, money, third parties, or moral claims.
- **Procedural:** focused on a defined deadline, notice rule, or exchange mechanic.
- **Mixed:** a legitimate request wrapped in one or more pressure devices.

Classify the message to choose a response structure—not to diagnose the sender.

### 6. Choose one disposition for every operative request

Use only what the facts support:

- `AGREE`
- `DO NOT AGREE`
- `NEED INFORMATION`
- `WILL COMPLY`
- `NOT APPLICABLE / NO ACTION REQUESTED`

Avoid “maybe,” “we’ll see,” or explanations that obscure the answer.

### 7. Add only the necessary boundary

Possible boundaries:

- `No other agreement should be inferred.`
- `Please send unrelated requests separately.`
- `My response to the request is complete.`
- `I do not agree that silence creates consent for this decision.`
- `I will follow the existing schedule unless we both agree otherwise.`

### 8. Apply the stop rule

Stop when the response contains:

1. receipt, if needed;
2. the operative request;
3. the disposition;
4. one necessary fact or rule; and
5. the next action, if any.

Do not defend your character, answer rhetorical questions, relitigate history, speculate about motive, or write for an imaginary judge.

## Response forms

### Universal closed response

> I received your message. Regarding the specific request to [request]: [AGREE / DO NOT AGREE / NEED INFORMATION]. [One necessary fact or next step.] No other agreement should be inferred.

### Bundled request

> I received the message. The operative requests appear to be:  
> 1. [Request]: [disposition].  
> 2. [Request]: [disposition].  
> Please send any unrelated new request separately. No other agreement should be inferred.

### Joint decision / non-consent

> I am responding within the stated period. I do not consent to [specific action]. I will follow the existing arrangement unless we both agree otherwise.

### Need information

> I cannot make a decision from the information provided. Please send [specific document/fact/proposal]. I will respond after receiving it.

### Unsupported obligation

> I do not agree that the obligation you describe is established. Regarding the concrete request to [request]: [disposition].

### Routine logistics

> Confirmed. I will [action] on [date/time].

### Repeat demand

> I answered this request on [date]. My response remains [disposition]. I have nothing further to add unless there is materially new information.

### Accusation plus request

> Regarding the concrete request to [request]: [disposition]. The remaining commentary is not necessary to decide that request.

## Deadline check

Before treating a label as controlling, ask:

1. Where does the deadline come from: order, agreement, provider, school, law, or sender preference?
2. What exact action is due?
3. When did the clock validly start?
4. What consequence does the controlling text actually state?
5. Is this subject excluded from assumed consent or unilateral action?

If uncertain, respond safely and on time while reserving the merits: `I received the request and do not consent. I am reviewing the applicable provision.`

## Evidence discipline

Keep an internal record using neutral fields:

- date and thread;
- exact operative request;
- governing provision;
- observable features such as bundling or scope expansion;
- your disposition and response time;
- outcome;
- corroborating record; and
- why the issue is materially important.

Avoid personality labels. Distinguish direct evidence, classification, reported effect, inference, and legal conclusion.

## Escalation ladder

1. **Handle in the message:** ordinary logistics and clear decisions.
2. **Log without debate:** recurring but low-consequence patterns.
3. **Ask a neutral professional:** ambiguous process or recurring coordination failure.
4. **Ask counsel:** rights, joint authority, material noncompliance, safety, or a repeated consequential pattern.
5. **Emergency channel:** immediate safety or medical emergency under the applicable rules.

Do not escalate simply because a message is irritating. Consider consequence, recurrence, proof, deadline, and available remedy.

## Low-cost outbound request

Do not let anticipated conflict eliminate legitimate requests. Use:

> Subject: [one concrete topic]  
> I am requesting [specific action].  
> Proposed details: [dates/times/cost].  
> Please respond by [real external or agreed deadline] because [brief reason].  
> If you do not agree, please state your disposition and one workable alternative.

## Final check before sending

- Is there a real question or decision?
- Did I answer every operative request?
- Is consent or non-consent unmistakable?
- Did I include only necessary facts?
- Did I avoid labels, motive claims, and character defense?
- Is the next action clear?
- Would deleting another paragraph make the record clearer?
