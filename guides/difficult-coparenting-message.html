Before you reply to a difficult co-parenting message

When a message makes your heart race, do not answer the whole feeling. Find the one question that needs an answer.

You may be looking at a long message with accusations, old arguments, and a request buried near the end. You do not have to respond to every sentence.

Use these five questions

1. What are they actually asking?

Write the request in one sentence. If you cannot, ask for the missing date, cost, location, or document.

2. Does it need an answer?

A direct question about a child, schedule, expense, or shared decision may need a reply. An insult or repeated accusation usually does not need its own answer.

3. Is the deadline real?

Check your court order, a school or medical deadline, or another reliable source. A message marked “urgent” is not automatically an emergency.

4. Who gets to decide?

Read the exact words in your parenting plan or court order. If the answer is unclear or important, ask your lawyer.

5. What is the shortest complete answer?

Try: yes, no, I need more information, or I will do this by this date. Add one next step and stop.

Example

“I can switch Saturday pickup to 3:00 p.m. Please confirm by Thursday at noon. I am not agreeing to a permanent schedule change.”
