Prepare for your divorce attorney meeting

Your lawyer can help more when the facts are easy to find and your questions are clear.

You do not need to tell the entire history in perfect order. Give your lawyer a short map, then ask the questions that require legal judgment.

Bring these four things

1. A one-page summary

List who is involved, where the case stands, the next known date, and the two or three decisions that feel most urgent.

2. A short timeline

Use dates and facts. Include only events that connect to the question you need answered.

3. The key documents

Bring relevant court orders, filed papers, financial records, and important messages. Label them by date. Ask the office what they want before sending large files.

4. A ranked question list

Put your most important legal question first. For each question, note the decision you need to make and any deadline you know about.

A useful opening

“The decision I need to make is ____. The deadline may be ____. These are the facts I believe matter. What does the law or my court order require, and what choices do I have?”

Keep practical and legal questions separate

Your lawyer is the right person for legal rights, risks, deadlines, and strategy. Organization, meeting preparation, and next-step tracking can often happen outside legal time.
